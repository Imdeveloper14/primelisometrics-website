Equations

As implemented, in the order the calculation runs.

— corresponding speed — λ = LS / LM Fn = V / √(g·L) VS = VM·√λ — strip friction off the model — CTM = RTM / (½·ρM·VM²·SM) CFM = 0.075 / (log₁₀ReM − 2)² CR = CTM − (1+k)·CFM — rebuild at full scale — CTS = (1+k)·CFS + ΔCF + CA + CAA + CR RTS = ½·ρS·VS²·SS·CTS — power — PE = RTS·VS ηH = (1−t)/(1−w) ηD = ηO·ηR·ηH PD = PE/ηD PB = PD/ηS·(1+margin)

What the terms mean

And where each one comes from.

CR — residuary coefficient
Everything that isn't skin friction: wave-making, plus form and viscous-pressure effects. Assumed to depend on Froude number alone, so it carries across from model to ship unchanged. This assumption is the whole method.
(1+k) — form factor
Accounts for the hull being a three-dimensional body rather than a flat plate. Measured properly by a Prohaska low-speed run; the auto value here is Watanabe's form estimate and is a stand-in only.
ΔCF — roughness allowance
The real hull is rougher than the varnished model. Bowden–Davison relates it to hull roughness and length.
CA — correlation allowance
The empirical fudge that reconciles predictions with measured trial results. Basin-specific — use your own if you have it.
Model Reynolds number
Below roughly 10⁶ the model boundary layer may stay partly laminar and the extrapolation breaks down. Turbulence stimulators (studs or a trip wire) are fitted to force transition.
This tool predicts calm-water, bare-hull resistance. Appendages, added resistance in waves, shallow water, wind and fouling are not modelled.
Model-basin extrapolation carries real uncertainty — typically a few per cent on power for a well-tested conventional hull, and considerably more outside that. Check the geometric-similarity warnings, confirm the model was turbulence-stimulated, and treat the correlation allowance as basin-specific before using any of this for design or contract purposes.
